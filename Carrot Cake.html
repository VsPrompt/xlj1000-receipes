Carrot Cake

A warmly spiced, lightly sweetened carrot cake built around blanched almond flour, a little coconut flour, fresh shredded carrot, and European-style butter. This version uses only the listed ingredients plus water and optional spices.

Prep: 15 minBake: 30–40 minCool: 30 minMakes: 8 slices

Ingredients

For one small 8-inch / 20-cm round cake

  • 2 cupsblanched almond flour (about 200 g)
  • 1 cupfinely shredded carrot, lightly packed (about 100–120 g)
  • ⅓ cupEuropean-style butter, melted and cooled (about 75 g)
  • ½ cupcoconut sugar
  • 2 tbspcoconut flour
  • ¼ tspbaking soda
  • ⅛ tspfood-grade citric acid (to activate the baking soda)
  • 2–4 tbspwater, as needed for a thick, scoopable batter
  • Optional½ tsp cinnamon, plus a pinch of salt and/or ginger if available
Important: This ingredient list contains no eggs or other strong binder, so the cake will be tender and more fragile than a conventional carrot cake. Let it cool completely before slicing. For a more reliable, springy cake, add 2 eggs if you have them; reduce the water to 1–2 tbsp.
Optional flours: Cassava flour or blue corn masa harina are not needed here. They change the texture, so leave them out for the best result with almond flour.

Instructions

  1. Prepare the oven and pan.
    Heat oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease an 8-inch round cake pan with a little butter and line the base with parchment paper.
  2. Mix the dry ingredients.
    In a bowl, whisk almond flour, coconut flour, baking soda, citric acid, and any optional spices and salt. Break up any lumps.
  3. Mix the wet ingredients.
    Stir melted, cooled butter and coconut sugar together. Fold in the shredded carrot and 2 tablespoons water.
  4. Make the batter.
    Add the dry mixture to the carrot mixture. Stir until evenly combined. Add more water, 1 tablespoon at a time, only until the batter is thick but scoopable. Avoid overmixing.
  5. Bake.
    Spread batter evenly in the pan and smooth the top. Bake for 30–40 minutes, until the top is set and the edges are golden. A tester may come out with a few moist crumbs, but not wet batter.
  6. Cool fully before slicing.
    Cool in the pan for 20 minutes, then carefully transfer to a rack. Allow to cool completely; almond-flour cakes firm up as they cool.
Texture cue: Coconut flour absorbs moisture quickly. If the batter becomes dry or crumbly, add water a tablespoon at a time. If it looks oily or loose, let it stand for 3–5 minutes before adding anything else.

Timing & serving

15 minPrep
30–40 minBake
8Slices

Allow about 1 hour 15 minutes to 1 hour 25 minutes from starting to serving, including cooling. Serve plain, or with a little softened butter if desired.

Storage

Because this cake is moist and contains fresh carrot, refrigerate leftovers in a covered container for up to 4 days. Bring slices toward room temperature before serving.
